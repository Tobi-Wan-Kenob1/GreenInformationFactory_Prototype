# Pre-registration — does the Policy & Grant Finder's ranking predict what was funded?

**Status:** final draft, ready to file · **Prepared:** 2026-11-20
**Protocol:** `paper/PROTOCOL.md` (this document is generated from it; where the
two disagree the protocol is wrong and gets a dated appendix entry, never a
silent edit)
**Registry:** OSF, public — not embargoed. See §9.
**Outcome measures computed so far:** none. That is the claim this document
exists to make checkable. Stated exactly, in §9.1.

---

## 1. What is being tested

The Policy & Grant Finder (`docs/finder/`) takes keywords, retrieves EU policy
documents and EU funding records, extracts topics across both corpora, and
ranks them. The question is whether that ranking carries information about what
subsequently happened, or merely describes the documents it was given.

The test is a **back-test**. The tool is run on a corpus frozen at a past date
*T*, its output is recorded and fixed, and only then is it compared against what
was funded after *T*. Nothing below depends on data from after *T* being seen
first, and the ordering is what makes the result worth anything.

**Not under test.** The tool's indicative CO₂ abatement and cost-saving figures
are assumption-based heuristics with declared parameters, not estimators fitted
to data. They are recorded for completeness and are explicitly outside the
hypotheses. The paper must not present them as validated. (Protocol §2, §9.1.)

## 2. Hypotheses and comparators

| | Hypothesis | Comparator | Seed set |
|---|---|---|---|
| **H1** | The tool's topic ranking predicts subsequent funding uptake | Random topic rankings, permutation test, 10 000 draws | Broad |
| **H2** | The full pipeline beats naive counting | Raw keyword document-frequency ranking | Broad |
| **H3** | Bridge topics (present in both corpora) outperform single-corpus topics | Within-run comparison of the two topic classes | Bridging |
| **H4** | Codes the tool flags as blind spots show lower subsequent uptake than covered codes | Within-run comparison | Bridging |

All four are directional. Each is tested against a comparator fixed here, not
chosen after the fact.

## 3. Design

Two freeze points, giving an internal replication.

| Arm | *T* | Input corpus | Outcome window |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A1** | 2009-12-31 | EU acts ≤ 2009, plus FP6 and FP7 projects started ≤ 2009 | 2010–2026 |
| **A2** | 2013-12-31 | EU acts ≤ 2013, plus FP6 and FP7 projects started ≤ 2013 | 2014–2026 |

The unit of analysis is the **topic**, not the organisation. That is where the
statistical power is: 122–145 distinct topics per arm and seed set in the
recorded output (§5).

**Availability at *T* is enforced in code, not by hand.** A CORDIS project may
enter an input corpus only if it *started* on or before *T*. The signature date
would be the better field and is empty in both FP6 and FP7, so `startDate` is
used; since signature always precedes start, the rule is conservative — it can
drop a project the tool could have seen but never admit one it could not.
Projects starting after *T* are outcome data and are excluded from the input by
construction, so the input and outcome sets are disjoint. (Protocol §4.2.)

## 5. Query selection

Queries are derived by rule from the BioFairNet WP1/D1.2 literature codebook
(`docs/finder/data/wp1_codebook.json`), which was produced independently of
this tool and predates the study. Neither the tool's author nor the analyst
picks them.

1. Take every code in the barriers and drivers dimensions.
2. Compute each code's document frequency in the frozen corpus, **separately
   for the policy and funding sides**. Discard any code matching more than
   **20 %** of either side.
3. Discard codes absent from the corpus; collapse codes with coinciding term
   sets, keeping the higher paper count.
4. Take the **ten** highest by codebook paper count; ties alphabetical.

Two variants, both fixed before any outcome data is retrieved:

- **Broad** — steps 1–4 as written. Seeds H1 and H2.
- **Bridging** — additionally requires the code to appear in **both** corpora.
  Seeds H3 and H4, because a term in only one corpus cannot produce a bridge
  topic at all.

Both are computed from the same frozen corpus in the same run, so neither is
selected after seeing the other's result. Matching is on a leading word
boundary, case-insensitive; the tool's own keyword thesaurus is **not** applied,
since it was written as part of the tool and would make the measurement
self-referential.

## 6. Outcome measures

Per topic, within the arm's outcome window:

- **Primary:** EC contribution (€) to projects whose title, objective or
  euroSciVoc classification matches the topic, **as a share of total programme
  contribution in that window**. Absolute euros are not comparable across
  FP7, H2020 and Horizon Europe, whose budgets differ by multiples.
- **Secondary:** project count; distinct participating organisations; count of
  subsequent EUR-Lex acts matching the topic.

Matching uses the same leading-word-boundary rule as selection.

## 7. Analysis plan

- Spearman rank correlation between tool rank and subsequent-uptake rank, with
  bootstrap confidence intervals.
- Permutation tests for H1 and H2 (10 000 draws).
- Mann–Whitney for H3 and H4.
- **Effect size reported in the abstract:** top-decile versus median uptake
  ratio.

**Significance is secondary to effect size.** With *n* in the low hundreds, a
small-but-significant correlation would not support a claim that the tool is
useful, and the paper will not present one as if it did.

Both arms are analysed separately and reported side by side. A result that
holds in one arm and not the other is reported as exactly that, not pooled
until it is significant.

### 7.1 Inference criteria, exclusions, missing data

- **Tests are one-sided**, in the direction stated in §2, at **α = 0.05**.
- **Multiplicity:** Holm–Bonferroni across the four hypotheses, within each arm.
  Arms are not corrected against each other; they are replications, reported
  side by side.
- **Confidence intervals:** 95 %, percentile bootstrap, 10 000 resamples.
  Random seeds are fixed in the analysis code and reported.
- **A hypothesis is supported in an arm** only if its corrected test passes
  *and* the effect is in the predicted direction. Support in both arms is
  reported as replicated; in one, as not replicated.
- **Exclusions:** none beyond those built into the corpus (§4) and the query
  rule (§5). No topic, project or act is removed after outcome data is seen.
- **Missing data:** a topic matching no outcome-window project has uptake 0 and
  stays in the analysis. A project with no recorded EC contribution counts
  toward project and organisation counts, contributes €0 to the primary
  measure, and the number of such projects is reported per arm.

### 7.2 From recorded output to the units analysed

Each recorded run is one query and holds three ranked lists — bridge, policy
and grant topics — plus the codebook coverage. A topic can recur across runs
with different ranks, so how runs combine is fixed here rather than chosen
later.

- **H1, H2 — ranked lists.** The unit is a ranked list: every (run, scope)
  list in the broad seed set with at least five topics. Per list, Spearman's ρ
  between tool rank and uptake rank; the statistic is the unweighted mean ρ
  across lists. H1's null permutes ranks within each list. H2's comparator
  ranks each list's topics by raw document frequency (policy + funding
  documents, as recorded); its test is a sign-flip permutation of the per-list
  difference ρ<sub>tool</sub> − ρ<sub>frequency</sub>. The number of lists
  used and excluded is reported.
- **Topic-level summaries** — the headline effect size and H3. Each distinct
  term counts once per arm, since its uptake does not depend on which query
  found it. Its rank is its best (lowest) rank in any list; it is a **bridge
  topic** if any run lists it under bridge, otherwise single-corpus.
- **H3** uses the bridging seed set: uptake of bridge versus single-corpus
  topics, Mann–Whitney, one-sided (bridge higher).
- **H4 — codes, not topics.** The unit is a codebook code, using the tool's
  full coverage computation (all 87 codes, not the 12 per dimension the panel
  displays). In the bridging seed set, a code is a **blind spot** if the tool
  marks it *not addressed* in every run, and **covered** if it marks it
  addressed on both sides in at least one run. Codes that are neither are
  left out of H4 and counted. A code's uptake is matched on its codebook terms
  by the §6 rule. Mann–Whitney, one-sided (blind spots lower).

## 8. Tier B case studies — illustrative, not confirmatory

Mechanism evidence only, labelled as such throughout the paper.

**Frame:** all organisations participating in projects funded under the
top-ranked topics in the arm's first three years — a complete register,
including the organisations that failed, which a web search for "who acted on
this" would silently drop. **FP7 onward only:** FP6's participant register is
1.5 % populated for organisation identifiers and 3.7 % for activity type, so it
cannot support sampling or tracing.

**Frame size:** 200–600 distinct organisations. *k*, the number of top-ranked
topics included, is not set directly but derived — accumulate topics in rank
order until the frame reaches 200, stop before it passes 600, and report the
*k* and frame size reached.

**Sampling:** stratify by CORDIS `activityType` (PRC / HES / REC / PUB / OTH)
and by country; *n* ≈ 10; **include every sampled entity that has since
dissolved**, up to a third of the sample, reporting attrition as a finding.
Enterprise size is **not** a stratum — the `SME` flag is empty in every
programme — and the paper must not imply the sample is balanced on it.

**No causal attribution.** 2009–2026 contains the financial crisis, COVID, the
energy price shock and CBAM. Tier B describes trajectories; it does not
attribute them to scenario alignment.

## 9. Openness, and what fixes the ordering

The study is conducted in the open: protocol, this registration, the frozen
corpora, analysis and results are all public as they are produced. The
registration is public rather than embargoed.

This makes the dates checkable by anyone, which is the point. **A registration
filed after the outcome data has been seen is worthless however it is dated.**
Working in the open makes that easier to verify and no easier to fudge: the
registration, the corpus manifests and the recorded tool output all carry
timestamps a reader can inspect.

**Order of operations, which is the substance of this document:**

1. Freeze the corpora; archive them with manifests. — *done* (`paper/data/`)
2. Select queries by rule; record every candidate's frequencies. — *done*
3. Run the tool; record ranked topics, bridge labels, blind-spot flags, for
   both seed sets. — *done*
4. File this registration; tag the repository `prereg-v1`. — **pending**
5. *Only then* retrieve outcome data and compute §7.

Steps 1–3 are complete and their outputs are in the repository under
`paper/data/`, each in a commit dated before this registration. Step 5 has not
begun.

### 9.1 What has and has not been seen

"No outcome data" would overstate it, so here is exactly where things stand.

- **Downloaded:** the CORDIS bulk files for FP5, FP6, FP7, H2020 and Horizon
  Europe, which contain the post-*T* projects. The freeze reads them and keeps
  only projects that started on or before *T*.
- **Inspected:** how completely their *fields* are filled — signature date,
  SME flag, activity type, organisation identifiers, across all programmes.
  That is what established, before any design choice depended on it, that
  `startDate` must stand in for signature date and that SME status cannot be a
  stratum (§3, §8).
- **Not done:** no post-*T* project has been matched to any topic or query; no
  EC contribution, project count or organisation count has been computed for
  any topic; no subsequent EUR-Lex act has been retrieved for the outcome
  window. None of the §6 measures exists in any form.

## 10. Deviations

Any departure from this plan once filed is recorded in a dated appendix to the
protocol with its reason, and reported in the paper as a deviation. Analyses
not pre-specified are reported as exploratory.

Changes made *before* filing are in protocol Appendix A, with the evidence that
prompted each. They are revisions to a draft, not deviations — but they are on
the record because the reasoning is part of the method. The substantial ones:
the policy corpus was found to be three-quarters routine market administration
and was rebuilt; the specificity filter moved from corpus-wide to per-side; and
the single seed set became two.

## 11. What this study cannot claim

- **The tool's metrics are not validated** (§1). At most their plausibility is
  bounded against published ex-post evaluations for a handful of projects, and
  reported as a sanity check.
- **The economic dimension is not firm-level.** No ORBIS/Amadeus licence, so
  firm accounts are out of reach. Tier B reports trajectory and survival, not
  profitability.
- **Mining has no regional economic series.** EU regional accounts resolve NACE
  only to 15 aggregates, burying mining in `B-E`. Mining is reported
  nationally; only agriculture can be reported regionally.
- **Scope:** agriculture and mining, EU level, English-language sources.
- **Retrieval quality is a secondary check**, not a hypothesis: two raters score
  ~100 returned documents for topical relevance, 60 each with 20 in common,
  Cohen's κ on the overlap, documents in randomised order with rank and topic
  class hidden. A low κ is reported, not re-rated.
